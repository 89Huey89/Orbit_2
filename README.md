# Orbit

Open **index.html** in a modern browser. The complete game, artwork, sound, and styles are inside that one file, with the seventeenth-century Fell typefaces embedded in `assets/fonts.css` beside it. No installation, server, network access, or build step is needed to play.

Tap or click to begin. While circling a node, tap to release along your current tangent. The next node captures you automatically. Space or Enter performs the same action. Tap after a loss to restart.

The dotted line shows your predicted trajectory. Pale ticks on your orbit mark clear tangent release points: skim the next orbit's rim so your incoming flight continues smoothly into the new orbit. A perfect preview includes a short curve showing the arrival direction. Near black holes, the guide bends and a bright mark appears at the current release point when the curved flight gives a perfect landing. The wider faint arc remains a forgiving capture window. Stay ahead of the rising dark and give black-hole centers room. Blue orbit rims mark drifting nodes; copper orbit rims mark nodes that fade 4.5 seconds after capture. Small gold nodes offer a harder bonus route.

Each of the four regions includes a fork: follow the wider orbits or take a narrower three-star constellation route. Both paths reconnect. The Needle, the Sail, the Lyre, and the Crown are marked in the star chart; stars light as you visit them. At a fork, two release marks indicate the two paths. Follow the gold links and capture all three marked planets to complete a constellation, earn 60 extra points, and make the darkness retreat for four seconds. Unfinished constellations expire when you pass their reunion point or an unvisited star sinks completely into the darkness. Each constellation can be rewarded once per run; your completed total appears at the end.

The opening speed is 150 world units per second, down from 205. Speed no longer rises automatically with progress. A slingshot star appears on the third planet, followed by the first constellation reunion and every eight main rows. Its engraved inner band fills as you orbit. One full lap adds 90 units of speed, up to a launch speed of 360; leave earlier for a smaller increase. Acceleration happens during the orbit, so releasing does not suddenly double your velocity. Each star provides at most one lap of acceleration. Waiting longer earns no extra speed or points, and pausing preserves your charge.

Earned speed carries between planets. A perfect transfer preserves the incoming velocity and joins at the closest point on the rim, including for a drifting planet. The game accepts a small band around the drawn orbit and keeps that arrival radius, avoiding a position or heading snap. A center-directed flight remains a forgiving ordinary capture, but the sharp turn removes up to 28% of speed above the opening pace. The guide and capture use the same moving-target solver. Flight launch speed is capped at 360 so repeated moving-planet assists cannot compound indefinitely. After the introductory star, each slingshot still offers its wider route and a direct transfer to a more distant planet.

Flights have no time limit: a long transfer continues until a planet captures you or you encounter a real hazard, the rising darkness, or the side of the chart. The aiming guide checks all generated destinations, including distant planets beyond its former 1.9-second horizon. Every full main orbit skipped earns an additional 10 points before the speed multiplier, so at the opening speed skipping three earns 30 and skipping seven earns 70. The landing message reports the skipped count and multiplied bonus; time in flight does not reduce the award.

Black holes have a local gravitational field extending 100 world units beyond their radius. Close flybys bend the airborne trajectory toward the hole, with slower and closer passes turning more strongly. This is a deliberately simplified arcade effect: the field steers the direction while preserving the player's chosen speed. The pull fades smoothly to zero at the edge, and it does not disturb an attached orbit. The dark center remains lethal; a close graze can still earn its once-per-hole bonus.

The curved aiming guide uses the same 120 Hz steering and swept collision rules as real flight, including moving planets and tangent-perfect arrivals. It stops at the first predicted capture, black-hole collision, or chart edge; a dangerous path ends in a copper warning mark. Prediction skips force-free straight sections, samples curves into a bounded polyline, and cannot award points or mutate the course. The long-transfer guide remains available.

Black holes lens the actual atlas background and starlight. A reusable 640-pixel canvas snapshots each visible region; concentric sampling bands stretch it outward near the dark center and settle smoothly at the field boundary. Fine tilted accretion rings retain the copperplate palette. Targets, orbit markers, the guide, and the player remain sharp and accurately positioned above the distortion. The subtle twist freezes on pause and is disabled for reduced motion, while the static lensing and gameplay pull remain.

The darkness starts advancing after 1.5 seconds at 22 world units per second, accelerating by 0.55 units per second of active play up to 150. Its edge stays near the bottom of the view while you ascend. The opening allows roughly ten seconds before idle play loses; later, consistently slow transfers become unsustainable. Speed-building stars and accurate transfers let the player stay ahead. The constellation reprieve still provides four seconds to recover or build speed, and pausing freezes the pursuit.

The rising darkness is painted as indigo-black ink soaking into the atlas: overlapping washes, pooled pigment, paper fibres, and fine suspended flecks. A thin copper shoreline sits at the actual loss threshold and becomes clearer as it approaches the player. Completed constellations turn the edge pale gold as it retreats, then the colour settles back as the reprieve ends. Both pigment plates are cached and reused across viewports. Pause freezes the wash, and reduced motion keeps its decorative grain and fringe still while the real boundary continues to advance.

Ocean worlds, cratered moons, ringed giants, fractured ice planets, desert worlds, volcanic planets, and storm giants each have their own procedural surface and size. Restrained verdigris, ochre, warm grey, chalk, and sepia washes sit on a paper-coloured base. Uneven pigment pools, dry-brush flecks, dark coastlines, concentric crater strokes, curved cross-hatching, and slightly offset outlines give them a hand-coloured copperplate character. Fine globe meridians and broken survey arcs accompany each specimen. Every seven main nodes include all seven families, in a run-dependent order, with individually seeded terrain and axial tilts. Planet appearance is separate from the orbit's behavior.

Etched surfaces turn slowly beneath fixed lighting and ring shadows. Cloud filaments drift separately; volcanic fissures gently brighten, and ice worlds have a faint polar shimmer. These decorative movements stop when reduced motion is requested or the game is paused. Surfaces, lighting, and weather are generated once per planet and reused as cached layers, with at most 24 planets retained.

The four chapters each have a full illustrated atlas plate. The Quiet opens beside a monumental engraved moon and a smaller companion. The Drift is crossed by a huge tilted ring system and a distant desert moon. The Eclipse has a dark solar disc with a corona of fine copper strokes and heavy bands of ink below. The Deep reveals a large spiral star field built from chalk-like contours and thousands of stippled stars. These distant scenes have distinct indigo, copper, violet-grey, and slate palettes, with a darker central channel for readable foreground play. Coordinate arcs and small plate captions tie them together. The brighter stars have eight-point compass forms.

Scene changes dissolve over several seconds and briefly reveal a large chapter name and plate numeral. Chapter lettering sits beside the play area on short landscape screens. Illustrated scenery shifts gently with the ascent behind two finer dust layers. Reduced motion keeps the decorative scenery still and removes the chapter lettering's small upward movement; pause freezes transitions. All four illustrations are cached at a fixed resolution, preserve their proportions across viewports, and use a separate random stream from gameplay. The entire game remains procedural and offline.

Quiet ambient events alternate between a small distant comet at the edge of the view and a brief glint on the moon, rings, corona, or spiral arms. Only one can be present at a time, followed by 19–30 seconds of quiet time. Events fade away near playable planets, hazards, the player, the aiming line, and rising darkness. Chapter reveals, constellation celebrations, recent captures, and immediate danger hold back new events. The ambient layer adds no sounds or interactions, uses its own seeded random stream, pauses with the game, clears on restart, and is disabled by reduced motion.

Each capture starts at 10 points. Releasing before 1.25 turns increases flow, up to ×5; each flow step adds 2 points per capture. A smooth tangent transfer adds 5 points, each full main node skipped adds 10, and a gold detour adds 15. These capture rewards are multiplied by arrival speed divided by 150, from ×1.0 to ×2.4, with the landing reward and skip reward each rounded to whole points. At twice the speed, the same transfer earns twice the points. The current multiplier appears below the score and on boosted landing rewards. Constellation completions remain a fixed 60 points, and grazing a black hole remains 5 once per hole. The existing best score and sound preference are preserved in local browser storage.

The player is drawn as a small engraved comet with a pale head, dark keyline, and fine asymmetric filaments. Its ink trail follows the actual flight path, tapers with age, and broadens subtly at slingshot speeds. Captures leave a broken etched ripple at the planet; perfect transfers add a second ring, survey ticks, and a small fan at the contact point. Effects pause with the run. Reduced motion shortens the trail and removes decorative filaments from the trail, particle bursts, expanding ripples, score movement, and screen flashes. Trail history stays bounded and is cleared on restart.

The game fills the available viewport and requests the browser's Fullscreen API on the first tap when supported. The corner control toggles fullscreen. On browsers without that API, including some iPhone browsers, the game uses the full available dynamic viewport. Switching away pauses the run; tap to resume.

Before the first tap, the title screen offers Relaxed, Classic, and Hardcore. The choice scales how quickly the rising darkness accelerates (×0.72, ×1, ×1.35) and is remembered in local storage for the next visit; every other rule stays the same.

A pale blue shield star appears roughly every ten main rows, off the main line like the gold detours. Capturing one arms a single charge, shown at the top of the screen and as a faint ring around the ship. The next contact with a black hole consumes the charge and deflects the flight outward instead of ending the run; only one charge can be carried at a time.

## Plates

The atlas can be printed on either of two plates, chosen with the page icon in the footer and remembered in local storage. The **night plate** is the original artwork: pale ink and starlight on indigo. The **paper plate** is the same chart pulled on aged cream laid paper, with iron-gall sepia and black ink, ochre where the night plate uses gold, rubrication red-brown where it uses copper, and Prussian blue for drifting orbits. The sheet carries laid and chain lines, fibres, foxing, old tide marks, and a dilute ecliptic with hour numerals. On paper the planets are hand-coloured specimens with an off-register keyline and hatched night sides, black holes are pooled ink blots, and the rising darkness is literally spilled ink bleeding up the page along the fibres. Every render section registers its colours for both plates in one place, so the night plate is unchanged by the paper work. Switching plates rebuilds the cached artwork and never touches the simulation.

Both plates sit inside an engraved frame: a plate-mark where the copper pressed the sheet, a heavy double rule, and a graduated scale with right-ascension hours along the top and bottom and declination degrees down the sides that scroll with the ascent. Wide screens add a compass rose, a scale bar, and an engraver's credit in the margins, outside the play channel. The frame is cached and costs one blit per frame. Orbit rings are drawn as burin lines rather than vector circles: each ring is baked once into a sprite whose stroke swells and tapers around the circumference, wobbles by a fraction of a pixel, skips in a few places, and carries a second faint hairline pass, then is blitted per frame. Lit constellation routes get the same doubled, variable-width treatment.

Lettering uses IM Fell English, IM Fell English SC, and IM Fell DW Pica, digitisations of the Fell types cut in the 1600s and used at Oxford, under the SIL Open Font License. The title screen is an engraved frontispiece with a strapwork cartouche, a Latin subtitle, and a printer's line; the end screen is a colophon with a ruled table of the run.

Each constellation fork carries the figure it is named for, drawn behind its three stars in the manner of Hevelius: a sailmaker's needle threaded through the stars, a lateen sail on its spar, a chelys lyre, and a jewelled diadem. The figures are broken ink contours that hatch in as stars are visited, take a dilute wash on completion, and fade to hairlines when a chart expires. Each is cached per chart and stays clear of the orbit rims.

The chapter plates quote Galileo. The Quiet's moon has a Sidereus Nuncius terminator with crater rims catching light on the dark side; The Drift carries his 1610 sketch of Saturn as a disc with two handles beside the caption; The Eclipse shows sunspot groups with hatched penumbrae across the disc; The Deep notes Jupiter and the Medicean stars as a small circle and four asterisks. Each plate has a second caption line naming the figure.

## Source

The independent `OrbitWorld` class handles seeded generation, fixed-step movement, swept collisions, scoring, and difficulty. The rest of the inline script handles procedural Canvas artwork, synthesized Web Audio, storage, and input.

Optional development commands use only Node.js built-ins:

```
npm test
npm run build
```

The verification script checks the main, constellation, and charged-shortcut paths on 60 generated courses through row 48, including 240 completed constellations and 360 shortcut transfers. It checks exact tangent contacts in both directions at three speeds, moving-target guide agreement, symmetric gravitational flybys, retained speed, curved landing predictions at three speeds, collision warnings, lens sampling at clipped viewport edges, star acceleration and caps, speed-based rewards, long-flight regressions, darkness pressure, rewards and expiry, duplicate capture protection, bounded generation, pause/restart, and complete-script execution at five viewport sizes using native-API stand-ins. Route probes can take an ordinary capture if they miss a perfect timing window for more than 1.5 turns. These are simulation/runtime checks, not browser screenshots. The build validates the script and copies the HTML and the `assets` folder into `dist` for static hosting.
