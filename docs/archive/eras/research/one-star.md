# One star, eight names — research file

The table behind JOURNEY.md §9.2's later rounds (settled 2026-10-01): every round of the Journey after the
first follows one real star through all eight centuries — round 2 the Pleiades, round 3 Sirius, round 4
Antares, round 5 Aldebaran, round 6 Vega with Altair (added the same day, KNOWLEDGE-AUDIT.md §3.2 item 8), then the
five again. The game's copy of this table is `ONE_STARS` in
`src/onestar.js`; this file is where each name is argued for. Labels are ERA-AUDIT.md's three:
**attested**, **plausible reconstruction**, **gameplay translation**. A name nobody can attest is never
supplied: the entry says that none survives, and the label then records what the game itself is doing
(standing a body in for the star).

Compiled 2026-10-01. Wikipedia and many primary-source sites are blocked from the sandbox this was
written in; what was checked, and through what, is listed under each star and in "What could not be
verified" at the end.

## How the names are set

- **I · The Rock** has no script. No name for any of the five survives; the line says so in English
  (`THE PLEIADES · NO NAME SURVIVES`) and the note hedges as the wall's own chart notes do.
- **II · The Ceiling** names only what the decan lists and the Senenmut ceiling securely give. Names are
  spelt in signs from the Ceiling's own checked sign set (`CEILING_G`) under the body, and in Latin
  letters in the inscription; the Egyptological ꜣ is avoided because no embedded face carries it.
- **III · The Scroll** gives the lodge (xiu) or star name in kaishu with pinyin, without tone marks, as
  the Scroll's charts already do.
- **IV · The Astrolabe** gives the Arabic in naskh with a plain transliteration, as `ASTRO_STARS` does.
- **V · The Atlas** gives Bayer's letter (Uranometria, 1603).
- **VI · The Lens** gives the Flamsteed number — numbers assigned from Flamsteed's telescopic catalogue
  (1725; the numbers themselves were added in Lalande's edition, 1783).
- **VII · The Flyby** gives the Hipparcos number (ESA's astrometry satellite, 1989–93).
- **VIII · The Probe** sets the star in its own log format, name and distance, as `PRB_SYSTEMS` does. The
  designations and figures are real; the probe's log is the game's, so every entry is a gameplay
  translation.

## Vega and Altair (round 6)

The Weaver Girl and the Herd Boy are a pair, and the pair is the point: two bright stars facing each other across
the Milky Way, which China made two lovers kept apart by the River of Heaven and al-Ṣūfī two eagles, one swooping
with wings folded and one flying with wings spread. The game's round, though, follows **one body**: the mechanism
deals one star per century, the store keeps one mask per star per century, and the descent letters one dot. Two
ways were weighed — a pair entry dealt on two bodies, or one entry that follows Vega with Altair in the notes — and
the second was taken. It keeps the mechanism and the store as they are, and it lets the sheet do what the pair
does in the sky: in the Scroll's and the Astrolabe's centuries Altair keeps its own name (the River Drum 河鼓, the
flying eagle) and may stand elsewhere on the same chart, across the sheet from the Weaver Girl. The entry's title
is the pair's (*Vega and Altair*, on the Record's page) and its `name` is Vega's (on the descent's dot). Vega rather
than Altair is the body because it is the brighter of the two, the Weaver Girl is the story's centre (she crosses,
or is crossed to), and it carries the deeper later history: Struve's parallax, the first stellar photograph, the
first debris disc.

### 河鼓, 牽牛 and 牛郎: which name is Altair's

The Scroll's office list (`SCROLL_OFFICES`, after the *Kaiyuan Zhanjing*'s three schools) holds both 織女 and
河鼓, and its chart notes already file Altair under 河鼓, "the River Drum holds Altair, the Herd Boy". That is
the right office, and the note in century III says so ("the great star of the River Drum"). The names need
keeping apart:

- **河鼓 Hegu, the River Drum**, is the three-star asterism in Aquila whose great star is Altair (in the later
  numbered list 河鼓二, "the River Drum's second"). Sima Qian: 牽牛為犧牲。其北河鼓。河鼓大星，上將；左右，左右將
  — "the Ox-Leader is the sacrificial victims; north of it is the River Drum; the River Drum's great star is the
  supreme general, those to left and right the left and right generals".
- **牽牛 Qianniu, the Ox-Leader**, in that same sentence is the *lodge* 牛 Niu, the ninth of the twenty-eight
  (around β Capricorni), south of the River Drum — a different asterism. The Scroll's `SCROLL_MANSIONS` has it as
  牛.
- But the name 牽牛 was also Altair's. The *Erya* (釋天) has 何鼓謂之牽牛, "the River Drum is called the
  Ox-Leader", and the commentators already disagree over it (Sun Yan: the River Drum's flag "lies north of
  Qianniu; some call it Hegu, and it is also called Qianniu"). The 牽牛 of the *Shijing*'s 大東, facing the Weaver
  Girl, and of the Han poem 迢迢牽牛星, is generally read as Altair: the name seems to have been Altair's first
  and to have passed to the lodge, leaving the bright star filed under the River Drum.
- **牛郎 Niulang, the Herd Boy**, is the folk-tale name for Altair in the Qixi story, not an office of the
  catalogue.

So the game sets 織女 for Vega (attested as both office and story name, unambiguous), and for Altair names the
office 河鼓 and the Herd Boy of the story, and does not set 牽牛 at all: on the Scroll's sheet that name would
read as the lodge Niu. In the round, `oneStarScrollTaken` stops the Scroll writing any other held body up as 織女;
河鼓 stays free, since it is Altair's own and its appearance elsewhere on the sheet is the pair, not a double name.
Likewise `oneStarAstroTaken` stops the Astrolabe naming a second body al-nasr al-wāqiʿ, while al-nasr al-ṭāʾir
stays in its pool.

The atlas's Lyra figure note ("Its brightest star is Vega, lettered α Lyrae after Bayer") names the figure, not a
body; the round's body is never a figure's star, so the two never letter the same light.

## What could not be verified

- No Wikipedia page could be opened; facts above were checked through search results quoting it and
  through the other sources named. The Flamsteed and Hipparcos numbers (25 Tau / HIP 17702, 9 CMa /
  HIP 32349, 21 Sco / HIP 80763, 87 Tau / HIP 21421) were each confirmed in at least one search result. For
  Vega and Altair (3 Lyr / HIP 91262, 53 Aql / HIP 97649) the numbers are the author's brief's, and agree with the
  standard catalogues as remembered; they were not re-confirmed online.
- The *Shiji* passage on 牽牛 / 河鼓 / 織女 was confirmed through search results quoting ctext and Wikisource;
  the *Erya* line (何鼓謂之牽牛) and Sun Yan's comment through results quoting the *Erya zhushu*. ctext itself
  is blocked from the sandbox, and the Shijing 大東 lines were seen only as quoted.
- Struve's Vega parallax (1835–36, published 1837), the Bond–Whipple daguerreotype (16–17 July 1850) and the
  IRAS debris disc (Aumann et al. 1984) were confirmed in secondary pages (astronomy.com; arXiv reviews) and
  not in the originals.
- Kunitzsch was not read directly; the Arabic names are the standard ones and agree with the
  Astrolabe's own table, which an earlier pass checked against him.
- The Khau = Pleiades reading rests on secondary summaries (Sparavigna; Sino-Platonic Papers 253); the
  primary decan literature (Neugebauer & Parker; Lull & Belmonte) was not opened. It is labelled a
  plausible reconstruction and the line carries a question mark for that reason.
- The hieroglyphic spellings of Khau and Sopdet are the game's phonetic spellings from its checked sign
  set, not copied from a particular monument.
- The Voyager 2 / Sirius and Pioneer 10 / Aldebaran figures were seen in NASA-sourced secondary pages,
  not on the JPL page itself.
- "Oculus Tauri" and "Palilicium" for Aldebaran come from Allen (1899) as quoted; whether Bayer's own
  plate prints either was not checked, so the atlas's line sets only the Bayer letter.
