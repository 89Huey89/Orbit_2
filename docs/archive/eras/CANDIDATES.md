# The eras not on the ladder

`OVERVIEW.md` sets a ladder of eight eras. This file is the honest record of everything that was
built, argued, or considered toward a ninth or tenth and did not make the cut — what each one is,
what already exists for it, where that work now lives, and what bringing it back would cost. A
candidate here is not a rejection: nothing described below was found wanting on its own terms. Each
was set aside because the brief that now governs this project asks for a specific eight-step
climb — one prehistoric era, Egypt straight into China, no Graeco-Roman era, no invented ninth
century — and a candidate is whatever that climb had no room for.

## The Disc

**European Bronze Age, c. 1800–1000 BCE** (the Nebra sky disc itself commonly dated c. 1600 BCE).
Held era II on the nine-era ladder; retired because the brief's roster carries exactly one
prehistoric era, and a second one — however well attested — duplicates the slot Era I already
holds. Read the full case in [`candidates/disc.md`](candidates/disc.md); the summary below is drawn
from it.

The Disc is the sky as a small object you could hold: a hammered bronze plate studded with punched
gold, built by a culture that tracked the sky with real rigor and left no writing behind at all.
Its controlling document is the Nebra sky disc itself (Landesmuseum für Vorgeschichte, Halle),
reworked across at least five phases over 150–200 years, alongside the Trundholm sun chariot, the
Nordic gold hats, the Tanum rock carvings and the razor corpus Flemming Kaul reads as one running
solar myth. Its grammar is unlike anything else on the ladder: no register bands, no size-coded
magnitude — every star is the same punched gold disc regardless of brightness, so knowing a star
means only knowing where it is. Its lettering section is the era's most honest finding: no script
existed in Bronze Age temperate Europe, so the disc counts by repeated identical punch-marks, a
tally rather than a notation, and every name the file offers a player is either a modern find-name
or an asterisked linguistic reconstruction.

**What was already done.** A full research file, [`research/disc.md`](research/disc.md); a
complete era write-up at [`candidates/disc.md`](candidates/disc.md) with palette, dangers, currency
rule and a signature sheet; a standalone prototype, [`prototypes/disc.html`](prototypes/disc.html),
which reached the standard on its second pass (`PROTOTYPES.md`), with a screenshot at
[`prototypes/shots/disc.jpg`](prototypes/shots/disc.jpg). All of it survives untouched and is named
in `OVERVIEW.md`'s own list of eras considered and not taken.

**The one thing that does not survive is the pairing.** A reconnaissance pass for this rewrite
proposed reusing the Disc's punch-and-gold-foil hand to skin era I's own transition object, so that
the Rock would hand off to the next era already wearing a recognizable Bronze Age surface. That
proposal is **refused**, and the refusal is a decision, not an oversight: the brief's cultural rule
holds that no tradition may appear as decoration inside another tradition's sheet, and a Bronze Age
punch-and-foil object is not Palaeolithic vocabulary — dressing era I's hand-off in it would make
the Rock's own transition lie about what century made it. What the pairing's prototype,
[`prototypes/turn-rock-disc.html`](prototypes/turn-rock-disc.html), leaves behind is worth keeping
regardless: it is the working mechanical study of a page turn itself — the inherited route re-struck
in a new hand, the old ground going dark beneath the new one's leading edge, the held body arriving
last with the new era's own ring around it — and every one of those beats is now general machinery
for the ten-step transition in `PROGRESSION.md`, independent of which two eras it happens to sit
between. What does not survive is the specific claim the prototype made about *which* two eras those
are.

**To bring it back:** slot it as its own era again, most naturally as a second prehistoric-adjacent
step before or alongside a Babylonian era (below), since its no-writing, one-punch grammar reads as
a genuine alternative to the Rock's mark-making rather than a repeat of it. All three of its research
artefacts are ready to receive it unchanged; only its position in a nine- or ten-era ladder needs
deciding, and the transition into and out of it will need its own prototype, since
`turn-rock-disc.html` no longer describes a hand-off this ladder makes.

## The Marble

**Graeco-Roman, c. 300 BCE – 150 CE, with a Carolingian afterlife to c. 816 CE.** Held era IV on the
nine-era ladder; retired because the brief's roster goes directly from Egypt to China, leaving no
slot for a Mediterranean era between them. Read the full case in
[`candidates/marble.md`](candidates/marble.md).

The Marble is the sky as a coordinate grid rather than a picture: the Farnese Atlas (Naples, MANN,
inv. 6374), a 2nd-century Roman marble copy of a lost Hellenistic original, carries 41–42 of the 48
classical constellations in unpainted relief crossed by engraved great circles — and **no stars at
all**, a hard constraint the file treats as load-bearing rather than a gap to paper over. Stars come
instead from a second document, the Mainz Globe, one of only three complete ancient celestial
globes; the era's grammar is explicitly a synthesis of the two, done in the open rather than
disguised as one surviving object. Its central intellectual inheritance is Ptolemy's six-class
*megethos* — the ordinal brightness scale of the *Almagest*, α′ through ϛ′ — which the file is
careful to separate from the era's grammar (textual, not visual) while keeping the classes
themselves as its one attested measurement.

**That inheritance is why the Marble is not simply gone.** `04-astrolabe.md`, this ladder's real
era IV, measures bodies into exactly the six-class scale the Marble's research already worked out
in full — the magnitude classes, their attested Greek names, the reddish-star exception
(*ὑπόκιρρος*) the *Almagest* gives six stars. `research/marble.md` therefore stays load-bearing:
it is not an artefact of a retired era but the sourcing underneath a live one, and any future work
on the Astrolabe's magnitude depiction should read it before inventing new Ptolemaic material.

**What was already done.** A full research file, [`research/marble.md`](research/marble.md); a
complete era write-up at [`candidates/marble.md`](candidates/marble.md) with three named grammars
(stone relief, engraved metal, painted parchment), a Greek/Roman lettering split, and a signature
sheet; a standalone prototype, [`prototypes/marble.html`](prototypes/marble.html), rated acceptable
from Sonnet with an Opus polish pass still owed (`PROTOTYPES.md`). All of it survives untouched.

**To bring it back:** the clearest slot is a Graeco-Roman era between the Ceiling and the Scroll,
where it would sit chronologically true to its own dates and would let the Astrolabe's magnitude
system arrive already dramatized once rather than only measured. Its own file flags the real open
problem first: no single surviving object shows figures, stars and colour together, so its three
grammars must be proven to read as one plate before it returns.

## The Babylonian planisphere

**Neo-Assyrian/Babylonian, c. 1000–500 BCE.** Never built — no research file, no era write-up, no
prototype exists for it. Argued for in [`ERA-AUDIT.md`](ERA-AUDIT.md), which calls it "essential"
and places it, in that document's own nine-era numbering, between the Ceiling and the Marble. Its
case rests on two attested documents of different kinds: *MUL.APIN* (British Museum tablet 86378,
ERA-AUDIT's own citation, is tablet I) — impressed clay, ruled columns, cuneiform star names,
rising and setting lists, and predictive or omen clauses — and the circular star map generally
called the Babylonian Planisphere, tablet K.8538, from the library of Ashurbanipal, which lays the
sky out as a diagram of wedge-shaped sky segments rather than a running list (unverified beyond
that description; no primary source has been checked for this file, and neither has ERA-AUDIT.md's
own MUL.APIN account, whose research it flags but does not confirm firsthand). Between the two, the
candidate offers something no other era on the ladder does: a sky recorded as an information system
— ruled, tabular, columnar — standing beside a sky recorded as a diagram, from a culture whose
astronomy is inseparable from omen and prediction.

ERA-AUDIT.md's own words on why this matters: "This is not a variant of the Nebra disc or Egyptian
ceiling; it is a new information and mark-making system." That claim has not weakened under this
pass — if anything the ladder's move to China for era III sharpens it, since a run that goes from
Egypt's painted register directly to a brush-drawn scroll now skips the one tradition in the ancient
world built openly around tabulated prediction.

**Why it has a clearer slot now than it did.** Under the old nine-era ladder this candidate had to
compete for space between the Ceiling and the Marble, both of which were already spoken for. Under
the brief's roster the Marble is gone and the Scroll (era III, the Dunhuang chart, 649–684 CE) sits
immediately after the Ceiling with a wide chronological gap behind it — Babylonian astronomy of
1000–500 BCE falls comfortably inside that gap, between the Ceiling's Egypt and the Scroll's Tang
China, without displacing either. That is a real opening this pass created, not a claim about
priority against the Disc or the Marble.

**To bring it back:** everything is unbuilt. A research file at the pattern `research/babylon.md`,
an era file, and a prototype would all need writing from nothing, following the era contract in
`ERA-AUDIT.md` (controlling object, universe model, observer ontology, body horizon, spatial
grammar, material and tool, lettering layers, feared sky, gameplay translation, negative rules) and
the evidence discipline the rest of this project holds to — attested, plausible reconstruction,
fictional gameplay translation, and "(unverified)" for anything not yet checked, which at present is
most of what is written about it here.

## The Dresden Codex

**Maya, Postclassic, the codex itself likely 11th–12th century.** Never built, for the same reason
as the Babylonian candidate: no research file, no era write-up, no prototype. Argued for in
`ERA-AUDIT.md` as "the strongest addition outside the Eurasian route" — bark-paper screenfold,
glyphs, figures, ritual calendars, Venus calculations and eclipse tables, with the holding
library's own content guide cited for the claim that Venus as morning star was feared as
misfortune and that the codex's pages picture struck victims. ERA-AUDIT.md's case for it is
specifically that it is the one candidate that supplies both halves of the brief's cultural
requirement at once: a visual system unlike anything else on the ladder, and a richly attested
feared sky, without inventing a danger mythology the way a thinner record would force.

Nothing in this pass changes that assessment or improves its slot. The brief's roster has no
American era and no natural adjacency for one; the Codex remains, as ERA-AUDIT.md left it, the
strongest candidate for whichever era comes after the ladder is next allowed to grow, alongside the
Babylonian planisphere above.

**To bring it back:** the same three artefacts as the Babylonian candidate, built from nothing, held
to the same era contract and the same evidence labels.

## What the roster did to two older candidates

**The Flyby is not a candidate any more — it is era VII.** ERA-AUDIT.md argued for 1965–1989 as
either its own era or an opening chapter of what that document called Observatory — the old plan's
own open question, unresolved because era VIII was, at the time, an observing era wearing a
travelling era's name. The brief settles it outright by demanding
a genuinely travelling era, and `07-flyby.md` is that era, built from
[`research/space-age.md`](research/space-age.md). There is nothing left in this slot to hold as a
candidate.

**The 19th-century lithographic era stays folded into era VI, as it already was.** Argelander's
*Bonner Durchmusterung* (1859–62) was never a candidate for its own era; it appears in
[`research/plate.md`](research/plate.md) "as an idea, not a method" — the moment figures gave way
to a grid of numbered dots, absorbed into what is now era VI's second register rather than granted
a sheet of its own. Nothing in this pass reopens that.

## The standard

A candidate is not a promise. Nothing above is queued, scheduled, or owed a build. An era earns a
place on the ladder in `OVERVIEW.md` only when it answers all five of that document's rules — the
simulation stays DOM-free and era-light; the era speaks in its own hand and borrows no other era's
lettering; it keeps the shared orbit and hazard vocabulary while revealing a body only as far as its
century could know it; it adds no dependency and no external resource; and the Observer Core sits
at its tool's working end unchanged. Until a candidate can be shown to answer all five, it belongs
here and not in the numbered files beside it.
