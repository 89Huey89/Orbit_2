# I · The Ceiling

**Egypt, c. 1479–1458 BCE.** The sky as it was painted on the underside of a tomb, where the
point was never to help anyone find a star.

## The documents

- **Senenmut's astronomical ceiling, tomb TT353, Deir el-Bahri** (XVIII Dynasty, reign of
  Hatshepsut, c. 1479–1458 BCE). The oldest known Egyptian celestial diagram. Divided into a
  **northern** and a **southern** half; the northern carries the earliest known list of the
  twelve months of the civil calendar, the southern the decanal star list with Orion and Sirius.
  A Metropolitan Museum facsimile by Charles K. Wilkinson is published and photographed.
- **Diagonal star tables** on Middle Kingdom coffin lids — the decans as a literal grid of
  thirty-six columns against thirty-six ten-day weeks. This is the era's real chart form: a
  **table**, not a picture.
- **The Dendera zodiac** (Ptolemaic, c. 50 BCE, now in the Louvre) — much later and already
  contaminated by Babylonian and Greek zodiacal signs, but the best-preserved circular Egyptian
  sky and the one everyone recognises. Use it as a chapter plate, not as the era's grammar.

## The grammar

Flat. There is no perspective, no shading, no light source and no attempt at one. The sky is
organised in **registers** — horizontal bands read in sequence — and figures are drawn in the
canonical composite view, each part from its clearest angle. Bodies are discs or figures, never
spheres. Nothing is modelled.

This is the era that most contradicts the engine's assumptions, which is why it is built last.

## Palette

Earth pigments on plaster, and only these: **Egyptian blue** (the first synthetic pigment,
calcium copper silicate), **red and yellow ochre**, **carbon black**, **gypsum white**, and
**malachite green**. Grounds are plaster cream or the deep blue of a night ceiling. There is no
gold in a painted ceiling — reserve gold for era II.

## Lettering

Hieroglyphs, in columns, with a transliteration beneath in the game's own smaller hand so a
caption is still legible as a caption. Unicode has Egyptian Hieroglyphs at U+13000, and
**Noto Sans Egyptian Hieroglyphs** is OFL-licensed — it can be embedded and run through
`scripts/glyphs.mjs` like any other face.

## Names

The era's own, not the Latin ones:

| The game's thing | Egyptian |
|---|---|
| Orion | **Sah** |
| Sirius | **Sopdet** (Sothis), whose heliacal rising opened the year |
| The Big Dipper | **Meskhetiu**, the Bull's Foreleg — the one certainly identified constellation on the Senenmut ceiling |
| The sky itself | **Nut**, arched over the chart |
| The ten-day weeks | the **decans** (*baktiu*), thirty-six of them |
| A chapter | a **register** |

## How the seven families are depicted

Flat discs with a painted interior, a heavy black keyline, and no shading whatsoever. Each family
becomes a **decan glyph**: an ochre disc for dune, a blue disc for ocean, a hatched disc for
storm. The ringed world takes the winged-disc form. Difference is carried by colour and by the
small figure painted inside the disc, exactly as a decan is identified in the tables.

## Chapter plates

1. The southern register of the Senenmut ceiling: Sah striding, Sopdet as a seated figure behind him.
2. Nut arched across the whole sheet, swallowing the sun at her mouth.
3. A diagonal star table — the raw grid of decans against weeks, drawn as the coffin lids draw it.
4. The Dendera circle, with its Egyptian decans and imported zodiacal signs together.

## Dangers

**Apep** for the attractor — the serpent of the underworld that swallows the sun, coiled flat in the
register. **The Eye of Ra** for the repulsor, **Shu** for the crosswind. Egypt is the era with the
least trouble here: its sky is *full* of named threats, which is more than can be said for its
bodies. See [DANGERS.md](DANGERS.md).

## Risk

The highest of any era. A flat, unmodelled, register-based sky is genuinely beautiful and
genuinely at odds with a game about depth and motion. Build a single chapter plate as a spike
before committing to the era.
