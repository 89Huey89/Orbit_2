# IV · The Astrolabe

**The Islamic Golden Age, 964 CE (al-Ṣūfī's catalogue) to 1437 CE (Ulugh Beg's).** The sky stops
being painted or ordered and starts being *measured*: a target sighted through two pierced holes,
an angle read off a graduated brass ring, a re-observed magnitude fixed to a name a scribe then
letters into a table built to be checked and re-checked, not merely believed. Where the Scroll
before it gathered lights into named relationships, this era gives one of those relationships a
number anyone with the instrument could reproduce — a different kind of knowing, not a better one.

## The documents

- **The Nastūlus astrolabe**, signed by Muḥammad ibn ʿAbd Allāh, known as Nastūlus, dated 315 AH /
  927–928 CE, made in Baghdad, now in the **Kuwait National Museum** (Dār al-Āthār al-Islāmiyyah) —
  the earliest *securely dated* astrolabe to survive, opening this era's own instrument tradition a
  full generation before al-Ṣūfī's catalogue below gives it something to measure into. Attested,
  cross-confirmed across independent sources; no shelf or inventory number was found (unverified).
- **ʿAbd al-Raḥmān al-Ṣūfī, *Kitāb ṣuwar al-kawākib al-thābita*** ("Book of the Images of the Fixed
  Stars"), composed 964 CE — Ptolemy's inherited magnitudes re-graded by al-Ṣūfī's own eye against
  the sky, not copied. **Museum of Islamic Art, Doha, MS.2.1998.SO**, dated 1125 CE, Baghdad, "the
  most authoritative copy" (Savage-Smith, 2013), is this era's manuscript register's primary
  object — demoted, under this file, from the sheet's main voice to its colophon vignette, but not
  removed: it is the catalogue every sighting on this sheet ultimately feeds.
- **The 1085 CE Valencia celestial globe**, Ibrāhīm ibn Saʿīd al-Sahlī, Museo Galileo, Florence —
  the oldest surviving Islamic celestial globe: brass, stars as small punched points, no colour
  anywhere on it. Exactly the grammar this file now leads with, not the vignette behind it.
- **Marāgha Observatory**, founded 1259 under Naṣīr al-Dīn al-Ṭūsī, near modern Maragheh, Iran — a
  library reported at roughly 400,000 volumes and, per its chief instrument-maker Muʾayyad al-Dīn
  al-ʿUrḍī, a mural quadrant of roughly 40 m radius and an armillary sphere of roughly 160 cm; the
  "roughly" is load-bearing, carried from secondary summaries rather than a primary text (attested
  institution and output, unverified radii).
- **Samarkand Observatory**, built 1428–29 under Ulugh Beg, its **Fakhrī sextant** trenched directly
  into a hillside, twin walls 40 m in radius — a structure keyed into bedrock so the arc cannot
  shift the way a freestanding brass instrument does. Its *Zīj-i Sulṭānī*, finished 1437, catalogued
  1,018 independently re-observed stars to a precision unmatched until Tycho Brahe a century and a
  half later. The correct closing document for the era.
- **Ibn al-Haytham, *Kitāb al-Manāẓir*** ("Book of Optics"), written c. 1027–1030s, the Süleymaniye
  Library's 1083 CE Ayasofya copy among the best known — pages dense with hand-drawn geometric
  construction diagrams: rays as straight lines, angles of incidence, compass-drawn circles standing
  in for the eye's own geometry. This era's other authentic chart, and the one its paper ground is
  actually drawn from.

## The grammar

Two grammars survive in the record and this file inverts which one leads. The old reading of this
era put the **manuscript page** first — al-Ṣūfī's painted figures, gold discs laid over them at
their catalogued places — and let the **instrument** appear once, small, as a colophon vignette.
The brief asks for the opposite, in its own words: avoid generic crescent-moon decoration, build
from astrolabes, alidades, angular measurement and engraved brass, and put the player inside an
alidade. So this sheet leads with **engraved brass**: a mater's raised, graduated limb; one or more
tympans cut with a specific latitude's altitude circles; a pierced, openwork rete carrying nothing
but a scatter of named star-pointers at their true positions, because the rete is otherwise total
negative space — the figure is gone, and only catalogued position and name remain; an alidade
pivoting on the front's own pin, its two pinnules the instrument's actual working end. Every line
earns its keep mechanically. Nothing on this sheet is drawn for decoration alone, which is the
sharpest possible answer to the brief's own warning about a crescent doing nothing but sitting
there.

The manuscript page survives, demoted rather than deleted, in two places only: as the **ground the
geometric construction sits on**, in the hand of Ibn al-Haytham's own optics diagrams — thin black
construction lines, lettered vertices, a compass-swing arc left visible, geometry meant to be
proved rather than measured from directly — and as a small **colophon vignette** in the corner of
the frame, carrying al-Ṣūfī's painted figure and its flat, unmodulated Baghdad-school colour exactly
as before, but no longer as the sheet's main voice. Never present anywhere on the sheet: linear
perspective, cast shadow, a painted night sky, or a crescent moon drawn for its own sake — the
Moon appears only as a body actually sighted, never as ornament.

but a *gradient of handling* — brightest where light, touch and gilding concentrate, darkest in the
grooves an alidade's edge never reaches — which is also, not incidentally, this era's frontier
(below).

## Lettering and the hand

Three scripts, right-to-left, doing three different jobs and never trading them. **Naskh** carries
running captions on both grammars, but reads differently on each: on the demoted manuscript page it
is the flowing book hand, the qalam swelling and thinning by drag angle, dots (*iʿjām*) added in a
second pass after the skeleton is drawn unbroken; cut into brass, naskh is instead small, cut and
angular — cartouche-scale, never the book-scale flow it keeps on paper. **Kufic** sets cartouches
and coin-like roundels on the manuscript ground and, on the instrument, the small neat inscriptions
a back plate carries, sometimes re-engraved later in a heavier hand when a piece was repaired or
updated (plausible reconstruction beyond the general naskh/kufic role split, which is attested).
**Thuluth** is reserved for anything monumental; no genuine OFL thuluth display face was found, and
this is carried forward as unresolved rather than papered over.

The brass introduces a fourth reveal primitive this era needs and neither `writeText()` nor
`penLettering()` supply, because a limb's graduation is not a script: the **struck division** — a
tick stepped off with dividers from a constructed reference circle, no glyph outline at all,
geometrically exact rather than penned, exactly the discipline a modern dividing engine automates
by machine. This is the motion the observation state actually draws (below); naskh and kufic only
enter once a reading is complete enough to be lettered beside it.

**Numerals split by surface, not by convention.** Manuscript prose — the HUD, the colophon, the
catalogue's own captions — sets Eastern Arabic-Indic digits (٠١٢٣٤٥٦٧٨٩), the numerals al-Ṣūfī's own
tables would have used. An engraved graduation — a limb, a shadow square, a completed ring — is cut
in **abjad numerals** instead: the twenty-eight Arabic letters, each carrying a fixed numeric value,
the system a real astrolabe's scale is actually marked in. The two systems appearing side by side
in one frame is a genuine legibility risk worth a small spike before the split is committed to the
glyph pipeline (below).

## Currency and the rule

**The *ḥibr*** — lamp-black scribal ink, rationed by weight the way the gold leaf beside it on the
demoted page was rationed. Nothing in the archival record prices a sighting by the degree, but a
calligrapher's wage and a folio's gold were both priced, in ink and metal, by weight, which is why
the currency stays ink under a new name rather than becoming some invented "measurement" unit — the
brief's own shorthand for this era, "the measured line," describes the *depiction* below, not the
economy. One rule governs all eight eras and this era carries no exception to it: traversing the
unknown spends *ḥibr*; a captured or documented orbit restores it; the dividend paid at release
scales by how much of the ring was actually struck before the player let go, so a hurried capture
banks less than a body measured through to its own completion. The depiction is the geometry
itself — the arc's own thickening tick marks are the visible ledger of *ḥibr* spent, never a
separate gauge laid over them.

**The old twice-drawn fork does not survive the reframe, and its retirement is deliberate rather
than an oversight.** The earlier draft proposed a constellation traced to completion on one branch
spawning its mirrored twin later in the run, echoing al-Ṣūfī's own convention of drawing every
figure once from the globe and once from the sky. It was always a spawn-scheduling rule, and a
spawn-scheduling rule cannot survive a sheet whose primary register is now an instrument's own
mechanical action rather than a teaching diagram's mirrored pair — there is nothing on an alidade
to mirror. It dies with the manuscript page's demotion, leaving the one rule above as the whole of
this era's economy.

## The bodies

Before an alidade is ever raised to it, a body on this sheet is an **unadorned point of light,
unsighted** — no engraved structure around it at all, sitting inside an orbit ring drawn at its
full, legible size the moment it enters the instrument's working range. That ring, its size, and
whatever class of body it belongs to for the purposes of flying a transfer to it are never staged
and never withheld: a run is lost here to a bad transfer exactly as on any other sheet, never to a
point the game chose not to show clearly. What the phenomenon withholds is identity alone — what
the sighting will actually find — which this era answers by instrument, in degrees, not by hand or
by name.

The instrument doing the answering is an **alidade**: a short, thin brass bar pivoting on the
sheet's own central pin, a small pierced sighting vane — a **pinnule** — fixed at its leading end
and a second, unlit pinnule closing its trailing end for silhouette balance, echoing the real
instrument's two-vane symmetry without competing for the eye. The **Observer Core sits at the
forward pinnule's own hole** — literally a hole drilled in metal with a point of light showing
through it, which is both mechanically exact (this is genuinely where a sighting's line of sight
passes) and the plainest possible rendering of the brief's own point that wants to know. The whole
rule *is* the tangent a flight will be judged against, and it is drawn visibly shorter than the aim
guide itself precisely so the two lines never fuse into one — an alidade laid along a sightline is
the one tool on this whole ladder whose real-world geometry already matches what Orbit's own pricked
guide draws, which makes them the single easiest pair of lines on the ladder to confuse if the
lengths are allowed to drift together.

What that sighted point produces is not a picture; it is a **measurement**, taken in the same
two-stage shape a real reading actually has — a raw angle at the back, resolved into a fact at the
front. At capture, a **thin engraved arc** begins to grow around the body: unlit, sparsely ticked,
the visual equivalent of an alidade being swept toward a target that has not yet been found. By a
quarter-turn the arc has thickened into a genuine partial protractor scale, ticks struck at even
intervals the way a limb's degrees are struck, still in the bare-incision colour rather than gilt,
because this is the *act* of measuring rather than its finished record. By half a turn the scale
reads substantially complete — most of the ring struck, only a short unlit arc still open. By
two-thirds of a turn the arc closes whole: the completed ring's ticks are picked out in gilt, and a small
abjad-numeral value is lettered beside it in naskh — the body's newly known *qadr*, stated the way
al-Ṣūfī himself stated a star's re-observed magnitude. The body itself gains, nested *inside* that
closed ring, the demoted manuscript grammar's own disc treatment — a gold disc sized to class, a
fine dark contour, a burnished rim — so the finished state visually nests one register inside the
other: the measured arc outside, the catalogued disc within, exactly as a sighting and a catalogue
are one activity carried out at two different scales. A body left before completion keeps an
honestly unfinished scale, gap and all, never a softened placeholder standing in for one.

The wanderers are sighted the same way, points among the fixed on an instrument that has never
drawn a sky, only measured points against one. The Moon is the crescent, sighted because a crescent
sighting is what actually sets this era's calendar — never drawn merely because a crescent looks
appropriately "Islamic," which the brief explicitly warns against. Al-Ṣūfī's little cloud remains
the one thing on this whole ladder that is not a point at all, and it is already where the obscurer
stands.

## The frontier

This era's word for the boundary is *aẓ-ẓulumāt* — "the darknesses," genuinely plural in the
Qur'anic and general Arabic usage it is drawn from, because more than one kind of record is
actually failing here at once. Engraved brass tarnishes, and it tarnishes *unevenly*: the gradient
of handling above — bright where an alidade's edge and a thumb actually work the metal, dark in the
grooves neither ever reaches — collapses toward one uniform dark patina, pooling first in the cut
lines and low relief and spreading outward until nothing on the instrument is being touched enough
to stay bright. The demoted manuscript ink fails by an entirely different mechanism: it does not
corrode through the page, it simply fades toward the page's own pale tone under light and slow
oxidation, so a construction line or a colophon caption loses the contrast that made it legible
rather than vanishing outright. Both are plausible reconstruction, extending
[research/instruments.md](research/instruments.md) §8's patina account and general manuscript-ink
conservation science; drawn as patina pooling and darkening outward from every incised groove on
the brass, and a slow desaturation toward the page ground on whatever manuscript survives beside
it, ending in the same place on both registers — geometry with nothing left to read it by.

The star behind a tarnished ring was there before an alidade ever swept across it and stays there
after the geometry measuring it has dulled past reading; what *aẓ-ẓulumāt* erases is only ever this
instrument's own account of having sighted it, which is why the flight can never turn back to a
reading already struck and lost.

## The assembling astrolabe

The background artefact this era fills, in place of a chart, is the **instrument's own geometry
assembling — rete, plate, limb, divisions** — built once, growing as the ledger grows, and read at
a glance rather than counted. Empty, it is a bare mater: an ungraduated brass disc, no plate seated
inside it, no rete laid over that, nothing engraved anywhere on the whole working surface. Half
filled, it reads as a real instrument mid-construction actually would: a tympan's altitude circles
partly incised, a handful of the rete's star-pointers placed and named while its openwork skeleton
is still mostly solid brass waiting to be pierced, the limb graduated in abjad numerals only part
of the way around its own circumference — an honest, uneven state of construction, never a
progress bar dressed as one. Complete, once this era's own ledger has armed, the stack is whole and
functional-looking: the rete's every named star-pointer set at its true position, the limb fully
struck end to end, the whole assembly reading, at a glance, as an instrument someone could actually
lift by its throne and sight a star through — the geometry that was, a moment before, scattered
engraving now closed into a working measuring device.

Once armed, the next main body the run has already been dealt is designated the transition object
and drawn exactly as any other body on this sheet — sighted the same way, its arc struck in the
same stages, captured the same way. What follows at that capture is *aẓ-ẓulumāt* run to completion
rather than to a shoreline: every ring this era ever struck, every gilt tick and every faded
construction line, tarnishes and fades all at once, while the transition object and the Observer
Core at the alidade's forward pinnule are the two things that failure is never allowed to touch. The
alidade itself then cross-dissolves into [The Engraving](05-engraving.md)'s quill over the same
second or two the world's forward advance is already easing for, the Core carried over unchanged at
the new tool's nib — and the body beneath it is not replaced but redrawn: the same star that was,
a moment before, a closed gilt ring with an abjad-numeral *qadr* beside it becomes a hatched,
lettered figure with a Bayer letter fixed to it, understood the atlas-page way rather than measured
afresh as though the astrolabe had never touched it.

This is not a lesser rehearsal for the printed atlas that follows it, and the atlas is not an
improvement on it. This is the one era on the whole ladder that turned a sighting into a number
anyone holding the same instrument could reproduce and check — a *qadr* that does not depend on
whose hand drew it or whose memory holds it, which is a kind of knowing neither the Rock's
remembered pattern nor the Ceiling's named order nor the Scroll's catalogued relationship could
offer, and one the Engraving's own lettered figures will inherit rather than surpass: the star Bayer
letters three centuries later was, first, a point an alidade's edge crossed against a graduated
limb, in a language of degrees rather than pigment, name, or place.

## Frame and furniture

The frame is not a border drawn around the instrument; it *is* the instrument's own mater and limb.
The plate-mark is the mater's raised outer edge, engraved in one continuous graduated ring in abjad
numerals rather than any European-style cartouche or scale bar; a small throne motif at the top
carries the HUD's own anchor point, its shackle standing in for whatever ring icon the run needs.
No RA/declination hour-ticks — this tradition lists and draws, table and figure side by side, never
one unified projected plate — and no crescent-moon flourish anywhere on the frame for its own sake.
A colophon-style end-of-run line closes it (scribe, place, date, on the original; player, plate,
seed, here), set beside — never inside — the small painted vignette carrying al-Ṣūfī's demoted
figure grammar, the one place on the whole sheet the manuscript page is allowed to be seen plainly
rather than as a nested disc.

## The signature sheet

First build: the **rete assembled around its own named star-pointers**, anchored on the two whose
very names still carry Orion inside them — *Rijl al-Jabbār* ("the foot of the Giant"), the Arabic
origin of Rigel, and the shoulder-star *Manqib al-Jawzāʾ* behind Betelgeuse's own name — each pointer
a small stylised bird's-head or leaf tip, engraved beside it in naskh, set at its true catalogued
position on an otherwise near-empty openwork skeleton. The demoted colophon vignette, in the
corner, carries al-Ṣūfī's own painted al-Jabbār from the Doha 1125 copy, small and flatly coloured,
so a player can see in one glance what this sheet used to lead with and what it leads with now.
Later enrichment adds: the 1085 Valencia globe seen close, brass with no colour at all; Marāgha's
mural quadrant, a fixed arc the size of a building; and Samarkand's Fakhrī sextant, its trench cut
straight into the hillside.

## Sound

A burin's short, dry bite into brass on capture, harder-edged than a quill on paper; the alidade
swinging free off its pin on release, a thin brass ring quickly damped; a divider's double click,
stepping a degree exactly onto a struck mark, for a clean tangent; a scratch that does not resolve
into a struck line for a graze that fails to land; gold leaf burnished in a soft, dry, rhythmic rub
for a body's own completion flourish; a dragon's dry hiss-and-coil, not the engraving era's liquid
pull, for the attractor's approach; and, at the frontier's edge, no ring at all — brass gone dull
enough to give back nothing when struck, the sound this instrument makes once no hand is picking it
up any more.

## The prototype

`docs/eras/prototypes/globe.html` exists and predates this reframe: it builds the earlier
manuscript-primary grammar throughout — sized cream paper, fine brown-black contour, flat colour
fills, gold discs for stars over the figure — with the instrument grammar appearing only once,
small, as a colophon vignette, exactly the arrangement §"The grammar" above inverts. Its header
records the choices made where the research offered options (*raʾs al-tinnīn* over al-Ghūl for the
attractor) and notes its own divergence from the decided pipeline: it relies on the browser's own
bidi/shaping (`ctx.direction='rtl'`, plain `fillText`) rather than fontkit's `ArabicShaper`, which
the build commits to instead, so it was already a fallback to verify against rather than the final
pipeline even before the reframe. Painter verdict on that manuscript-primary build: Opus reached the
standard on the second pass. **No spike yet exists for this file's own instrument-primary grammar**
— the brass mater/limb/rete assembly, the alidade avatar, the struck-division reveal state — and
that gap is this era's own largest open risk, not a settled one.

## Risk

The alidade's own geometry is the era's hardest single legibility risk: a real alidade laid along a
sightline is already what Orbit's pricked aim guide draws, so the two lines must be visually
distinguishable — by length, by material, by weight — before any other art for this era is
committed, exactly as [OBSERVER-CORE.md](OBSERVER-CORE.md) already flags. The abjad-versus-Eastern-
Arabic-Indic numeral split is a real, useful detail but an untested one on screen at once, and
deserves its own small spike before it is built into the glyph pipeline. Arabic shaping remains
tractable with a known tool but real, scoped work, and should be spiked — one full caption
pre-shaped via `font.layout()`, its outlines stroked cleanly through `penLettering`/`writeText` —
before any other asset for this era is built. The dragon-on-a-throne depiction is invented and
flagged as such: the concept and the general motif are attested, the specific placement is not. The
Oxford collection figures, the Marāgha instrument radii, and the extra Samarkand architectural
figures all rest on secondary summaries this pass could not independently re-verify and should be
traced to a named primary source before being presented in player-facing copy as more solid than
"roughly." A genuine OFL thuluth display face is still missing. And because no instrument-primary
prototype yet exists, the single largest unknown is simply whether the brass grammar reads as
clearly, at speed, in motion, as the manuscript-primary draft already proved the painted grammar
could — that has to be spiked, not assumed.
