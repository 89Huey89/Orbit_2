# VIII · The Probe

**The far future.** No era follows this one. A self-replicating interstellar probe, long after
crewed astronomy, long after any human eye ever again rests at an eyepiece or waits on a downlink
— the ladder's own end, reached not by inventing a grammar out of nothing but by watching every
earlier instrument's own reach for permanence complete itself in a machine built to keep looking
once nobody is left alive who asked it to. Where every era before this one handed its sky, its
tool and its unfinished argument forward to the century that came next, this one has nowhere
further to hand anything on to: what it does that no earlier tradition could is finish the climb,
and then keep exploring anyway, on a road the game no longer measures in centuries.

## The two ends rhyme

Era I struck marks into stone because nothing else durable was available to a species with no
metal, no kiln and no writing. Era VIII is a species that has since invented radio, lasers and
machine memory — and when it needed a message to survive a billion years of vacuum, it *still*
chose a metal plate and a single continuous engraved line, because nothing it had built since
actually outlasts a burin cut once and never re-inked. That plate, its rhyme with the Rock's own
dab, and the burin-line grammar that draws it belong to **The Flyby**, the era that actually
launched it: the Pioneer plaque, the Voyager Golden Record cover and the CCSDS telemetry transfer
frame's own sync marker are its documents now, not this file's, and whichever hand The Flyby
settles on for engraving them is the hand this era inherits rather than redesigns. What this file
keeps of the old argument is only the rhyme itself, restated one link further down the chain: the
first era marks a rock because nothing else in its century would last long enough to be found
again; the last sends out a machine built to outlive the civilization that made it, carrying,
bolted to its hull and copied unread into every daughter that follows, the same plaque The Flyby
cut once. Two acts of the same instinct — commit the sky to a medium that survives its author —
aimed across the whole span of the ladder at each other, and this era only has to notice that, not
invent it a second time.

## The documents

This is the one era on the ladder with no cave wall, no ceiling, no chart, no plate and no primary
image of its own to research — its documents are the engineering literature a self-replicating
probe would actually be built from, not an object anyone photographed:

- **Freitas, "A Self-Reproducing Interstellar Probe"** (*JBIS* 33, 251–264, 1980) — REPRO, the
  namesake of the whole idea: a probe that reaches a target star, drops a SEED, and lets SEED grow
  into a FACTORY able to mine the system and build a second REPRO. **Attested.**
- **NASA/ASEE, "Advanced Automation for Space Missions"** (1980 Santa Clara summer study; NASA
  CP-2255, 1982) — the self-replicating lunar factory chapter, the nearest thing in this
  literature to a costed bill of materials, and the source for treating **closure** — a factory
  able to build every one of its own parts — as a threshold crossed once rather than a flag that
  simply flips. **Attested.**
- **Von Neumann's universal constructor** (posthumously published, 1966) and **Bracewell's 1960
  contact-probe variant** — the two poles the whole idea has stood on since: a replicator that
  explores by building more of itself, and one that explores by listening instead. This era
  follows the first; the second survives only as a plausible late-run event, a probe that goes
  quiet and starts transmitting, never the core loop. **Attested** as a structural idea.
- **Tipler (1980) and Sagan & Newman's 1983 reply** — the one attested danger built into an
  unbounded-growth currency: exponential replication is fast enough to saturate a galaxy in a
  geological eyeblink, and the reason it has not happened is exactly the reason a run's own bill
  keeps rising rather than staying flat as generations climb. **Attested.**
- **Project Daedalus** (1973–78) and its revival **Project Icarus** — a fusion-pulse design
  fuelled by helium-3 mined from a gas giant's own atmosphere by a fleet of aerostat platforms, the
  one design in this literature where the mining *is* the mission's gravity well. **Attested**,
  not independently re-verified this pass.
- **Breakthrough Starshot** (2016–) — a gram-scale, laser-driven lightsail probe, the attested
  real-world analogue for the reflector's own sail subsystem. **Attested**, final spec
  **(unverified)**.
- **The asteroid-mining and ISRU consensus** — C-, S- and M-type compositional classes, ice-moon
  and ring-ice water, and lunar/Martian regolith's aluminium, silicon, iron and titanium: the basis
  for the four-material harvest below. **Attested**, cross-source consensus rather than one paper.

Every choice past this list is **fictional gameplay translation**: nobody has built REPRO, and
this era is speculative fiction resting on real proposals, not on an object anyone has actually
flown. The discipline that keeps it from becoming an invented era with nothing under it is that
every choice below traces back to one of the sources here, flagged wherever this pass could not
check it further.

## The grammar

Two grammars share this era and never merge, but only one of them is authored here. **The
telemetry half** instruments the sky rather than drawing it: a body is numbers before it is a
shape — mass, density, a periapsis pair, a class tag — an orbit a Keplerian element set plotted as
a thin conic section, black ground, monochrome vector lines, no shading, no perspective. This half
is this era's own, evolved past a mission's downlink into something closer to a ship's log:
nothing here is addressed to Earth any more, so it keeps the fixed-field, fixed-cadence shape of a
transfer frame without the ground-facing header a real one needs — a virtual-channel ID, a sync
marker meant for a receiving antenna — because there is no antenna receiving it and nothing on the
far end to synchronise with.

**The plaque half is not this era's document.** It is the single continuous engraved line The
Flyby actually cuts — the same one Freitas's REPRO carries as cargo — bolted to the hull and
copied into every daughter along with the rest of the blueprint, unread by anything the probe has
ever met and unedited by anything this era does. A player should be able to tell the two halves
apart without a caption: one ticks and updates, the other has not changed since it was cut. Not
present, in either half: perspective, atmosphere, painterly shading, or any picture of the sky *as
seen* — the probe has no eye, and everything shown is either a measurement or an inheritance.

radiator's glow and starlight.

## Lettering and the hand

Two registers, and — for the first time on the ladder — neither is authored fresh here. **The
plaque hand** is The Flyby's own engraved line, and whatever face that era settles on for it — the
Hershey stroke-font family is the strongest candidate, single-stroke vector data with no contour
to flood, needing a code path closer to `writeText()`'s clip-reveal than `penLettering()`'s
stroke-then-flood — this era draws it exactly as cut, never redrawn, never re-lettered, because a
copy that improved on its ancestor would no longer be the same plaque. **The self-log hand** is
this era's own: a monospace face built for the job, **B612 Mono** (OFL, designed for Airbus
cockpit legibility under vibration and glare — still, on this ladder, the single most on-theme
face available), **Share Tech Mono** and **DSEG** (OFL, seven-segment LCD/LED emulation) as
fallbacks. A feedstock counter that ticks like an odometer suits a machine with no hand left to
write with, and no digit here is ever set by a stroke — every number updates, the way an
instrument reads rather than the way a scribe writes.

## Currency and the rule

Per [ECONOMY.md](ECONOMY.md) and [OVERVIEW.md](OVERVIEW.md)'s ladder, the currency is **mass** —
feedstock and reaction mass mined or scavenged from a body's own material, never the brief's own
guess of an abstract "energy," because this era's harvest already commits it to four named
materials rather than one unit, and because mass is, uniquely on this whole ladder, also the
literal substance of the next generation. The traversal rule underneath it is the one rule every
era now shares, unrewritten: traversing the unknown spends capacity, a landing and a held orbit
return it, the dividend scaled by how documented the departing body was left. The probe's harvest
does not sit beside that rule as an exception; it is a second write at the same credit sites,
reading every gain the table already pays — a capture, a perfect capture, a held orbit — a second
time into the material of the body that paid it. A half-documented ice world therefore yields half
the volatiles a fully documented one would, for the same reason it now yields half the mass: the
harvest reads the number after the completeness multiplier, never before it.

Mass sorts into four materials, few enough to hold in one head: **volatiles** (ocean, ice, ringed —
water- and organic-rich bodies, the single most commonly attested space resource), **silicates**
(crater, dune — structural aluminium, silicon and titanium), **metals** (volcanic — nickel-iron and
platinum-group ore, the exposed core of a differentiated body), and **fusion fuel** (storm —
helium-3 and hydrogen, Daedalus's own Jovian harvest). The four pickups become finished parts
rather than raw stock: the slingshot a salvaged drive core, the shield a pre-fab radiation-shield
plate, the reflector a sail segment, the inkwell a concentrated isotope cache. Material choice is
never a decision — it is read off a body's family, the one thing that keeps a one-button game one
button.

A daughter probe costs a bill of the four materials, four of each provisionally, tuned to land
about as often as a constellation completes today. The bill accrues from ordinary play; nothing is
inserted into the chart and `this.random()` is never called again to meet it. When it is met, a
second craft peels from the traveller's own line toward the top of the frame, the score takes a
flat bonus scaled by generation, and the darkness grants the same grace a constellation does. `GEN`
rises by one. The first bill met is **closure**, recorded once as a medal, after Freitas and the
NASA lunar-factory study, in which a replicator is closed when it can build every one of its own
parts; every bill after is replication proper, larger than the last. The run never forks — one
traveller, one score, always — and a daughter's own departure is the score's growth, never a second
probe the player ever flies.

## Dangers

All three rows keep their existing field/core/reach/lethality; only depiction and naming change.
This era's own risk is drawing an instrument grammar so close to its two nearest neighbours — the
Lens's third register and the Flyby — that a row reads as a repeat of a plate the player has
already seen earlier the same run:

| Row | Name | Depiction |
|---|---|---|
| Attractor | **WELL** | An equipotential mesh, a warped grid converging on the mass, given as a number rather than a disc — a mesh, never a ring, so this row and the Lens's own rendered EHT disc never read alike. |
| Repulsor | **BEAM** | A pulsar's swept beam or a flare's radiation front, a rotating sector wedge — a reading, not the Lens's coronagraph image or the Flyby's own radiation-belt band. |
| Crosswind | **ISM FLUX** | Thin monochrome flux-vector arrows, no colour gradient — the row closest to repeating the Lens's solar wind and whatever the Flyby settles on for the same row; the fix is register, not idiom. |
| Obscurer | **EXT** | A shaded attenuation region with a logged magnitude of signal loss, not a rendered nebula. |

## The bodies

Before a sensor is ever aimed at it, a body on this sheet is a **sensed mass, its class
unconfirmed** — a return strong enough to fix a position and a capture-region size with total
confidence, exactly as every earlier era's phenomenon does, but carrying no more identity than a
number on a screen. Nothing a trajectory depends on is ever staged here either: this is the same
rule the whole ladder answers to, expressed in the one era with the least excuse to bend it, since
a probe's own sensors could trivially know more than they show and simply choose not to say it yet.

The instrument doing the withholding is a **von Neumann probe**, and per
[OBSERVER-CORE.md](OBSERVER-CORE.md)'s own table, **the Observer Core sits at its replication
core** — not at a sensor boresight, not at a dish, but at the one component every daughter must
carry intact for there to be a next generation at all. Every other tool on this ladder places the
Core at the point that resolves an image or completes a measurement; this one places it at the
point that survives being copied, which is not a choice among several plausible seats the way the
telescope's eyepiece or the astrolabe's pinnule was — a self-replicating machine's core must
outlast every copy for the fiction to hold together, and the game's own rule that the Observer
Core is one of exactly two things every transition carries across uncut is, at this last tool, the
identical requirement rather than two that merely happen to agree. It is drawn omnidirectional,
forward only by convention, because a replication core has no leading edge to speak of.

What that sensing produces is not a picture, and — for the only time on the ladder — not quite a
document either: a **readout**, ticking in digit by digit as the orbit is held. At capture, a
spectral read resolves first, a rough class guess with wide error bars. By a quarter-turn a mass
figure and a mean density have locked, and the class icon — the plaque's own reductive
line-and-tick shorthand, borrowed as an inheritance rather than redrawn — begins to resolve around
the readout. By half a turn a periapsis/apoapsis pair has been struck, the reading substantially
complete. By two-thirds of a turn the last digit locks and the class tag prints in full, sealed by the
ticking counter simply going still — this era's own small flourish, a machine's silence standing
in for a stroke it cannot make. **Understood**, at completion, is the sensed mass, the readout and
the material at once: a body autonomously catalogued and priced for the harvest, with no
privileged Moon and no exception — every body on this sheet, for the first time on the whole
ladder, is read the identical class-first way, because there is no naked eye left aboard to see one
of them differently from the rest.

This is the knowledge gain the whole ladder was climbing toward: **place → autonomously explored
world.** Say it once, here, plainly: what every earlier era's own dab, decan, catalogue entry, gilt
ring, lettered figure, resolved sphere and mapped place was always quietly building toward was
this. The faint recurring lights the Rock's hand kept circling in the dark were never merely
lights. They were worlds the whole time, exactly as real as the one under this probe's own
sensors, and every century in between simply lacked the instrument to know it. This is not this
era outdoing the seven before it. Nothing it reads was hidden from them by distance or by
darkness; it was hidden by the tool each of them had to work with, and each of those tools found
everything it was capable of finding. What autonomy adds is only that the finding no longer needs
anyone left alive to receive it.

## The frontier

This era's word for the boundary is **the flux**, already settled in
[ECONOMY.md](ECONOMY.md): a probe is not chased by night, it is chased by its own ageing —
radiation damage, thermal budget, a reactor's half-life — and time in transit is the whole of the
cost. The rate, the grace and the shoreline are exactly what [THE-FRONTIER.md](THE-FRONTIER.md)
fixes for every era, unchanged. What actually fails behind the player here is the one substrate on
the whole ladder with nothing physical to decay: not a mineral, not a binder, not an emulsion, not
a signal path, but a **bitstream**, and a bitstream does not flake, tarnish, fade or drop out — it
flips. Accumulated radiation over the timescales this era implies causes single-event upsets, bit
by bit, until an error rate finally exceeds what the frame's own error-control field can still
correct, and a block does not decay toward anything: it reverts, all at once, to a flat,
textureless null or error pattern, because there is no image underneath a corrupted bit for a
texture to fade into. Drawn as the least textured decay of all eight — a hex or hatched block
flattening to one uniform value, on a machine's own cadence rather than a hand's. **Plausible
reconstruction**: single-event upsets and checksum failure are real, attested phenomena; their
application to a probe this far in the future is fictional gameplay translation.

This is the correct place on the ladder for this decay to land. Every material above it — ochre,
plaster, ink, brass, paper, an emulsion, a signal — leaves some residue of what it was on its way
to nothing: a scar, a stain, a dropped tile that at least proves something was once there. Data
does not. What the flux erases behind this probe is only ever this era's own account of a body it
has already left, exactly as every earlier frontier only ever erased an account and never the star
behind it — but here, for the only time, there is nothing left standing in the ash to show a
record ever existed at all.

## The manifest

The background artefact this era fills is the **manifest** — not a chart of the sky, since every
body on this sheet is already fully legible as a phenomenon before it is ever touched, but the
schematic of the next daughter, assembling from the ancestral plaque outward as the bill above is
paid. Empty, at the opening of a bill, it is bare: the plaque's own inherited outline traced faint
at the centre, no subsystem drawn in around it. Half filled, it reads as a real bill partway met: a
drive core sketched in where a slingshot has been salvaged, a shield plate outlined where one has
been fitted, silicate structure pencilled around both while metal and fuel are still logged as
zero — an honestly uneven build, never a bar standing in for one. Complete, once the bill is met,
the schematic closes into a whole second hull, indistinguishable in outline from the one that built
it, the ancestral plaque copied onto it in miniature exactly as the original was cut.

Here is where the brief's own gate stops applying rather than merely changing shape. Every earlier
era's ledger arms once and hands the run to the next century; this one has no next century to hand
to, so completing the manifest does not arm a transition — it launches the daughter described in
"Currency and the rule" above, `GEN` rises by one, and the manifest simply clears and starts again,
tracing the same inherited outline from zero for the bill that follows. The knowledge structure
every other era fills once, this one fills over and over, for as long as the run survives to keep
paying for it — which is the truest picture the ladder can offer, at its own end, of a species that
has stopped needing to finish anything in order to keep going.

## Where the ladder ends

There is no era IX, and this file does not pretend otherwise. Reaching this era's own transition
object — the last one the game will ever designate, captured exactly as every other body on every
other sheet has been — completes the historical climb permanently: no ninth century waits to be
grown outward from the body under the pen, because there is no further pen to hand the point to.
The Observer Core does not cross into anything at that capture. It simply keeps drawing, on the
same tool, in the same era, because the era itself does not end.

What happens instead is what the whole ladder was climbing toward without saying so until now: the
run does not stop, it escalates, on machinery the game already owns rather than any new one built
for the occasion — larger transfer gaps, more complex orbital configurations, stronger
gravitational anomalies, higher speeds, stranger bodies, more exotic fields, the same row-driven
scaling that has been quietly running since row one, simply no longer capped at the point this era
used to be the end of the story ([PROGRESSION.md](PROGRESSION.md)'s own endless mode). This is
where high-score play has always actually lived, and it is the run's real length: seven eras of
climbing to earn the right to a probe that keeps exploring, and then as much of the game as a
single flight can survive after that. Every era before this one redrew the same sky in the
language of its own century, no rendering an improvement on the one before it, each complete in
the terms available to it. This is the one era with no next century left to hand its own redrawing
on to, and it keeps going anyway.

## Frame and furniture

No cartouche, no colophon, no engraver's credit — the closest equivalent bolted to the hull is the
inherited plaque's own identifying marks, The Flyby's document, not this era's. The frame is a
**self-log block**: a running block counter and a local elapsed-time tally in place of a year,
since the far future keeps no calendar a caption could set a date against, closing on the same
checksum whose failure is the frontier's own decay above — the frame's structural feature and its
point of failure are, deliberately, the same field. No virtual-channel ID and no antenna-lock
margin: those addressed a ground station this era no longer answers to. In their place, a margin
strip carries `GEN` and the manifest's own fill state, the only two figures this probe has any
reason left to keep.

## The signature sheet

**Chosen: closure.** The first bill met, the medal struck once, a second craft peeling from the
traveller's own line toward the top of the frame on an `ESC` burn, the ancestral plaque's engraving
repeated in miniature on its departing hull — the moment this era actually earns its place at the
ladder's end, rather than merely arriving at it. Later enrichment: a manifest reading half-built
mid-bill; sail deployment against interstellar black, the hull reduced to a bright point at its
centre; a later generation's departure, `GEN` ticking past its first small numbers into whatever a
surviving run reaches.

## Sound

A steady low reaction-wheel hum, rising under load, replacing the quill-scratch of flight; a
self-check chime that only sounds while the probe's own memory reads back clean, its dropout —
silence, not an effect — the loss cue in place of a signal getting lost to anyone on the other end;
a short two-tone frame-sync chirp on every `OI`; a tone climbing and cutting off cleanly for `ESC`;
a sparse mechanical tick marking elapsed time under the flux's rising pursuit; a low chime, once,
on closure.

## The prototype

`docs/eras/prototypes/probe.html` draws bodies as low-detail lit spheres wrapped in their data
rather than the research's untested icon-plus-readout pair, hand-builds the plaque hand as
single-stroke polyline glyphs rather than loaded Hershey data (a licence-and-pipeline question this
file now defers entirely to The Flyby, which owns the plaque), and chooses instrument white over
telemetry green, the brief's own explicit "no CRT-green cliché" call. It loads B612 Mono (OFL)
locally and draws THE ARGO as its constellation, a ship apt for a probe under sail. Its treatment
of the plaque as this era's own document predates this pass's handover of it to The Flyby and
should not be read as still current; its self-log hand, its class-icon-plus-readout bodies, and its
palette choices survive that handover untouched. Painter verdict: Sonnet reached a prototype
standard; budget Opus for whichever era ends up drawing the plaque. See
[PROTOTYPES.md](PROTOTYPES.md).

## Risk

The manifest's own repeating-rather-than-arming behaviour is this file's one genuinely new
mechanical idea and is unbuilt and unspiked; check it against the same `verify.mjs`
score-distribution probe [PROGRESSION.md](PROGRESSION.md) proposes for every other era's ledger
before trusting the cadence. The handover of the plaque, the record and the CCSDS frame to a file
that does not yet exist is a real dependency, not a formality — if The Flyby settles on a different
engraving hand than Hershey, or a different frame convention than the one sketched here, this
file's palette and lettering sections need a pass to match rather than merely to check. Overlap
with the Lens's third register and with the Flyby remains the biggest visual risk on this end of
the ladder, and the register choices above (mesh, wedge, flux-arrow) are untested side by side with
either neighbour. The chapter cap, `chapter=Math.min(3,Math.floor(world.progress/8))`, has to lift
past this era for the endless run to exist at all — an engineering item
[OVERVIEW.md](OVERVIEW.md) and [PROGRESSION.md](PROGRESSION.md) both name, not a design question
this file can settle. The four-material harvest, the bill's exact size, and `RELEASE_FLOOR`'s own
value are all provisional per [ECONOMY.md](ECONOMY.md) and need the same built-sheet tuning every
other era's constants do. Build this one last, and build it knowing nothing after it has to agree
with anything it does.
