# II · The Ceiling

**Egypt, c. 1473–1458 BCE.** The sky as a painted order of hours, months, decans, moving lights
and protective beings, on Senenmut's astronomical ceiling in tomb TT353. Nothing here is a pyramid,
a scarab or a pharaoh's cartouche for its own sake — the tradition on trial is specifically Egyptian
**astronomy**, and every decision below is checked against that line before it is checked against
anything else. Where the Rock before it could only strike a mark and return to it, this is the
first sheet on the ladder to give the sky a formal, named order: a decan seated in its own hour, a
wanderer gathered into a family of restless gods that "know no rest." That is what this tradition
did that the Rock's memory could not; it is not an improvement on the Rock, only a different
instrument put to the same watching.

## The documents

- **Senenmut's astronomical ceiling, tomb TT353, Deir el-Bahri** (XVIII Dynasty, reign of
  Thutmose III, c. 1473–1458 BCE) — the controlling source. Two stacked panels on a **light painted
  ground**: the northern panel's twelve month circles, each divided into twenty-four (read at
  closer resolution as thirty) segments, the circumpolar *ikhemu-sek* ("the imperishable ones")
  and four named wanderers, Jupiter, Saturn, Mercury and Venus, gathered as the *ikhemu-wretju*
  ("the stars that know no rest") — Mars is omitted; the southern panel's decan-name columns, Sah
  (Orion) striding, Sopdet (Sirius) seated behind him with her star, the hippopotamus Reret holding
  the mooring post that tethers Meskhetiu, the Bull's Foreleg, so Set cannot roam the northern sky.
  Held at the Metropolitan Museum as Charles K. Wilkinson's public-domain facsimile (acc. 48.105.52,
  H. 73 × W. 56.5 cm, roughly 1:5 of the ceiling itself); the Ancient Egyptian Astronomy database at
  McMaster records this ceiling explicitly as `Nut: No` — no Nut arch belongs on this sheet.
- **Diagonal star tables ("star clocks")**, Middle Kingdom coffin lids, predominantly Asyut with
  single examples from Thebes, Gebelein and Aswan, c. 2100 BCE — decan names against the
  thirty-six ten-day weeks of the civil year, read diagonally: the era's one genuine table, no
  figures at all, and the strongest evidence that "Egypt" and "a grid of nodes" are not in tension.
- **Ramesside star clocks**, royal tombs KV6, KV1, KV9, c. 12th century BCE — a roughly
  twelve-by-seven grid of stars keyed to the body parts of a seated figure, "upon the heart," "the
  right eye." The single most useful document here for gameplay, not merely for look: a literal
  target grid, the closest attested thing on this ladder to the game's own tangent/release
  geometry, kept as a later comparator rather than composited into TT353.
- **The Book of Nut**, from the Osireion at Abydos and the tomb of Ramesses IV — a different,
  later diagram type, informing this era's research without licensing a Nut arch on TT353's own
  sheet.
- **Greenfield Papyrus, frames 85 and 87** (British Museum, c. 950–930 BCE) — a black-line solar
  barque and a Nut-Shu-Geb vignette, roughly five centuries later than TT353. They license two
  borrowed motifs — the barque's flat profile silhouette, Shu's crosswind grammar — never a claim
  that either appears on the ceiling itself.
- **The Dendera zodiac** (Ptolemaic, c. 50 BCE, Louvre D 38 / E 13482 / CM 464) — a later, already
  syncretic circular sky incorporating Babylonian and Greek signs; a single accent plate, never
  this era's grammar.

## The grammar

Flat. No vanishing point, no gradient, no cast shadow, no atmospheric depth — structurally absent
from every document above, including the latest and most sophisticated, Dendera. Figures stand in
the canonical composite view (frontal torso, profile head and legs, frontal eye); bodies are signs,
discs or divine figures, never little rendered planets. Two grammars coexist without being mixed:
the **figure-register grammar** of TT353 and the Book of Nut, deities and animals standing for
constellations at a fixed scale unrelated to the sky's own geometry; and the **table grammar** of
the diagonal and Ramesside star clocks, no figures at all, rows and columns doing the whole of the
work. The game's own node lattice fits the second without translation; the moving figures fit the
first. Both reject a spatial starfield, and neither is a limitation this era lacked the geometry to
escape — the snapped red cord and the eighteen-square canon (below) are real, precise construction
tools, aimed at register and column rather than at depth by choice, not by want of instrument.

The hierarchy matters more than the swatches: roughly three quarters light plaster, most of the
remaining information fine black, then small red, yellow, blue and green islands. Egyptian blue is
a valuable pigment in the wider Egyptian repertoire but is **not this ceiling's background**; gold
leaf and orpiment do not license a generic gold UI, and the built sheet uses muted yellow ochre in
their place.

## Lettering and the hand

The historical layer sets in locally bundled **Noto Sans Egyptian Hieroglyphs** (OFL 1.1),
registered as the face token `hiero`. The face supplies the shape; the renderer supplies the hand —
every sign is painted through the wall's own four documented passes, a **red setting-out laid off
register, a thin black correction, a flat colour flood, and the black closing line last**
(`research/ceiling.md` §4), each sign doubled a hair off register where the brush reloaded, so no
two signs are identical and none reads as type. Signs are stacked in **quadrats**, one, two or
three to a square — the layout that makes a column read as writing rather than as a list of
pictures — and the columns beside the route spell only words the checked vocabulary can spell in
full; a wall this dense would rather stay quiet than invent an inscription. The English layer sets
in a slab serif (Zilla Slab, OFL 1.1), the class the trade named "Egyptian" in the 1810s after the
Napoleonic revival, and is kept out of every historical column so it never poses as a translated
tomb inscription. The score stays an Arabic numeral in a cartouche for instant play readability; a
count the wall itself makes — the hour, a small tally — is struck in Egyptian numerals instead.

## The player and the Observer Core

**The player is a rush brush, not the solar night barque.** Cut from a rush stem (*Juncus
maritimus*, not true reed) and chewed at the tip until the fibres fray into a soft, flat brush —
attested New Kingdom practice, c. 1567–1085 BCE (`research/ceiling.md` §4) — this is the actual
instrument a scribe-priest held for monumental and tomb-wall work, distinct from the reed *pen*
(*calamus*) of the later Roman-period hieratic tradition. Its active end is the frayed fan of
bruised fibre, not a point: the fan leads the flight flat-on, the one tool on the whole ladder with
no aerodynamic vane at all ([OBSERVER-CORE.md](OBSERVER-CORE.md)'s table). The Observer Core sits at the wet tip of
that fan — a bare disc, argument-free, exactly `markHead()`'s shared contract: it never dims,
brightens, pulses with speed, or points anywhere. Every reading of "how much *khar* is left" is
carried by the brush's own bead, the wet pigment core already drawn by `ceilingWet()` for the wall's
setting-out marks, drying pale as the ration runs low and rewetting dark at a capture — a reading
that belongs to the tool, never to the Core beside it.

This costs the code exactly one function. `ceilingDrawPlayer()` (`src/ceiling.js:1350–1438`, some
ninety lines) currently draws Ra's night barque — hull mirrored to face the direction of travel,
eased frame to frame, never inverted, a scarab-panelled naos carrying the solar disc, two steering
oars crossed at the stern — and it is the only function this pass touches. The remaining roughly
1,700 lines of `src/ceiling.js` — the wall bake, the register grid, the four hazard painters, the
route, the reveal, the running head — are untouched, exactly as [OBSERVER-CORE.md](OBSERVER-CORE.md) and
[OVERVIEW.md](OVERVIEW.md)'s build order both promise.

The barque does not leave the level. It is a real and specific object in the tradition this era is
built from — the vessel that actually carries the sun and the circumpolar gods through the night —
and that is exactly the register it returns to: a moving light drawn as a tiny profile barque (see
"The bodies," below), backed by Greenfield Papyrus frame 85's flat silhouette, never again the hull
the player steers. [CEILING-POLISH.md](CEILING-POLISH.md)'s P12 ("keep the barque level; let the course turn") and P13
("let the crew do something") retire outright rather than migrate to the brush: both were fixes for
a hull silhouette that had to be kept from reading upside down through a full orbit, and a fan-tipped
brush read flat-on has no stern to invert and needs no oar to prove it is alive. Nothing analogous is
owed the new tool by inheritance; whatever liveliness the rush brush earns is this pass's own small
design task, not a debt carried over from the barque.

## Currency and the rule

Per [ECONOMY.md](ECONOMY.md), the era's currency is **the *khar*** (ḫꜣr) — the fixed monthly grain ration paid
to the Deir el-Medina tomb-builders, the best-documented wage in the whole record and a truer fit
than the paint itself, which the record barely prices at all (`research/ceiling.md` §6). This is a
rename only. The traversal rule is the one rule every era now shares — traversing the unknown
spends capacity, a landing and a held orbit return it, the dividend scaled by how much of the
departing body was documented — and the Ceiling changes none of its numbers, only its word and its
depiction. What a player reads is the brush's own bead, wet and dark when the *khar* is full,
drying pale toward `CEILING_WET`/`CEILING_DRY` as it runs low, rewetted at a capture. The
every-tenth-row grain dividend once floated for this era (`research/ceiling.md` §6) is retired along
with every other era-specific twist [ECONOMY.md](ECONOMY.md) drops; the *khar* is earned and spent on the same
schedule as every other era's currency.

## Dangers

The named beings below answer the historical brief — danger as people imagined it in the sky — but
their assignment to four force mechanics is a fictional gameplay translation; the depictions obey
Egyptian image grammar, the physics do not claim an ancient source.

| Row | Name | Depiction |
|---|---|---|
| Attractor | **Apep** | The serpent that ambushes the sun god's bark nightly in the underworld, a recurring threat survived rather than defeated — coiled flat within a register, its coil tightening as the pull takes hold. |
| Repulsor | **The Eye of Ra** | The scorching solar disc sent out as Sekhmet, or simply radiating uraei — "pushes outward, small lethal core," survivable at range, dangerous only close in, its rays flaring as the player closes. |
| Crosswind | **Shu** | The god of air, physically holding Nut and Geb apart — drawn kneeling with raised arms, currents drifting along their own axis, a myth that already describes "holds things at a steady distance." |
| Obscurer | **Nun** | The formless waters before creation, everywhere and nowhere — an inert fog patch that hides rather than harms, its hatch drifting as a travelling wave rather than sitting fixed. |

## The bodies

Under [KNOWLEDGE-HORIZON.md](KNOWLEDGE-HORIZON.md) this era draws no planetary family and no surface — no Egyptian
document on this ladder differentiates a body's face — but it does draw the plan's three states,
though the retargeting that drives them from the swept arc rather than from the moment a mark
scrolls into view is not yet built. Today `reveal.progress()` keys the wall's four passes to a fixed
1.25-second timer started the instant a body enters the top of the sheet (`NODE_REVEAL`,
`src/reveal.js:24,105–115`) — the same chart-draws-itself-in convention every era shares, and
identical for a body the player has never touched as for one just captured. What is already built,
and is the model [KNOWLEDGE-HORIZON.md](KNOWLEDGE-HORIZON.md) and [OVERVIEW.md](OVERVIEW.md)'s build order both name outright as the one
to generalise rather than replace, is the four-pass stage itself: `ceilingPolygon()`'s `stage`
argument already splits into quarters — a red setting-out first quarter, a black correction second,
the colour flood third, the closing black line last (`src/ceiling.js:192–217`) — a mechanism built
for a scroll-in timer that maps onto the swept arc without new geometry, only a different number
feeding it.

Read against that retarget, the three states on this sheet are: a **phenomenon**, before capture —
a faint star, or a dim disc, a ghost of a register position, its rim and size already exactly what
the understood body will keep, since position, capture-region size and any gameplay-critical class
are never staged and never withheld, only identity is. An **observation**, while the orbit is
held — at capture, the red setting-out is snapped as one construction line, off-register, exactly
the way the wall is drawn out under every mark on the sheet; by roughly ninety degrees swept, the
black correction pass has fixed a silhouette, and the sign reads as one of its three kinds — a
plain star sign, a star mounted on a huntite disc, or a wanderer's tiny barque — with no colour
committed yet, which is exactly what "recognisable as its kind" asks for; by roughly a hundred and
eighty degrees, the colour flood is complete; by roughly two hundred and forty, the closing black
line shuts the sign and, where a decan or a wanderer stands beside a column, its name is lettered in
full. The sign's own hub ring, already laid last and only once a mark is nearly finished
(`ceilingStar()`, `stage>.78`), is already the wall's small completion flourish — a lock closing
over the arms' shared point, precisely the one honest instant [KNOWLEDGE-HORIZON.md](KNOWLEDGE-HORIZON.md) asks a
completed body to carry, with no gauge beside it. **Understood**, at completion: a named, ordered
element of the cosmic order — a decan seated in its hour, or one of the four attested wanderers
gathered into the *ikhemu-wretju* — never a picture of a surface this tradition never claimed to
see. TT353 names Jupiter, Saturn, Mercury and Venus and omits Mars; the sheet therefore treats the
barqued sign as the class mark for a wanderer generally, never a false one-to-one catalogue of five
named planets drawn onto random gameplay nodes.

**The frontier**, following [THE-FRONTIER.md](THE-FRONTIER.md), is called **isfet** (jzft) here — the disorder
ancient Egyptian religious thought set against *ma'at*, cosmic order, a fictional gameplay
translation of an attested concept, kept distinct from Set's own domain in the Storm family. What it
consumes behind the player is this era's own record failing by two separate mechanisms, neither of
them the brief's own gesture of fading pigment read too literally: the secco binder — gum, glue or
egg medium — breaking down so pigment loses cohesion and powders on its own; and, independently,
groundwater salts crystallising beneath the plaster and pushing it from its support in blisters that
widen into flakes, the dominant failure across Egyptian tomb conservation generally. Drawn as pale
efflorescent bloom first, then flat matte voids in blocky, faceted shapes where plaster has lifted
away — never a soaking wash, which is the visible difference from every ink-based era on the ladder.
**Plausible reconstruction**, general conservation science; no TT353-specific conservation report
was reachable this pass ([THE-FRONTIER.md](THE-FRONTIER.md)).

**The knowledge structure** the era's own observation ledger fills is the decan table Senenmut's
ceiling already lays out — concretely, the twelve month circles of the northern panel, each already
built as a ruled box waiting for a caption line and the red construction rule struck through its own
centre (`ceilingMonthCircle()`, `ceilingMonthBox()`). Today all twelve are baked once, statically,
captioned wherever the checked vocabulary has a word to spell and left honestly blank where it does
not; what the plan asks is to gate that same caption-by-caption arrival on the era's ledger rather
than drawing every circle finished from the start. Empty, at the era's opening, every wheel stands
as a bare spoked circle with no name beneath it, all twelve alike. Half-filled, some wheels carry a
lettered caption under a red construction rule and the rest still stand bare, exactly the mixture the
wall already tolerates for a word its own vocabulary cannot spell. Complete, when the ledger arms the
era's transition, every wheel that has a name to carry wears it — the wall's own progress display,
with no bar or percentage drawn beside it anywhere.

## The turn into The Scroll

None of [PROGRESSION.md](PROGRESSION.md)'s ten-step transition is built for this era yet — there is no observation
ledger, no designated transition object, and the standalone `src/ceiling.js` preview is not wired
into any era-transition machinery at all. What follows is the plan, stated as such. The transition
object completes in this era's own hand exactly as any other body does — a decan seated in its hour,
or a wanderer's barque, finished through the four passes above — and then isfet is allowed to run to
completion across the whole sheet rather than only the band behind the player: the register grid, the
month circles, the kheker frieze, Apep and the Eye and Shu and Nun, all of it lost to the same
plaster-loss and pigment-powder described above, run once rather than to a shoreline. Two things
survive it. The transition object remains, because the next era is about to grow outward from it.
The Observer Core remains, unconditionally, because its draw call was never conditional on era to
begin with. The rush brush then cross-dissolves into whatever The Scroll's own sighting instrument
turns out to be — [OBSERVER-CORE.md](OBSERVER-CORE.md)'s roster names it an armillary sighting tube, barrel laid along
the tangent — over the second or two the transition's own eased advance allows, the burin-to-brush
technique run once more in the opposite direction. The same body, the same Observer Core, a
different hand.

## Frame and furniture

A tomb ceiling has no frame in the atlas sense — its boundary is the room's own architecture, and
its furniture is the register grid itself plus hieroglyphic captions beside every figure. The
**cartouche** (*shenu*, "that which encircles"), the oval ring around a royal name, is a genuine,
structurally simple element and the natural home for a player's initials or best-score readout; the
running score and best are set inside one. The civil calendar — season (*Akhet*/*Peret*/*Shemu*),
month, day — is a real dating formula and a better fit for the daily plate's date line than a
generic "day N." Rim captions become decan-name columns beside the main orbit, as on the ceiling
itself. There is no colophon in the modern sense; a scribe's dedication text is the closest
equivalent and a weaker fit than era VI's imprint line, probably not worth forcing.

## The signature sheet

**TT353 as one authored sheet** — the circumpolar group, decan columns, the four attested moving
lights, omitted Mars, and twelve lunar-month circles laid out from the facsimile rather than freely
recomposed. Ramesside star clocks and the Book of Nut belong to separate, explicitly later Egyptian
studies; they must not become extra "chapters" inside a purported reconstruction of TT353.

## Sound

A grinding pigment-grind on the orbit hold, a single wet dab at capture, a dry brush-flick at a
perfect release, a low stone thud at death, a sistrum rattle at a completed course — all five built,
per [CEILING-POLISH.md](CEILING-POLISH.md) P14.

## The prototype

`docs/eras/prototypes/ceiling.html` exists and paints the southern-panel scene; it is superseded as
colour authority by the direct facsimile inspection recorded in `research/ceiling.md` and above — any
dark-blue night reading or Nut association did not survive the comparison. The playable
implementation is `src/ceiling.js`, entered temporarily from the main menu outside any era ladder.
[CEILING-POLISH.md](CEILING-POLISH.md) records all nine of its ranked proposals as built: the wall now carries with the
climb and bakes a register per watch (P1/P2), the running head is retired in favour of the arriving
register (P3), the wall has a visible brush and drying edge (P4), red setting-out survives under
every finished mark (P5), the flown route is loaded dabs rather than a flat line (P6), a snapped cord
and a dropped plumb stand in for the atlas's survey (P7), a capture has its four distinct marks back
(P8), floaters are set as marginal notes (P9), the three hazards breathe and pull (P10/P11), the era
has its own five sounds (P14), and — retired outright by this pass rather than carried forward — P12
and P13, which existed only to keep a barque hull from inverting through a full orbit and are moot
now that the player is a rush brush with no stern to invert. Painter verdict on the original
prototype: Opus reached the standard on the second pass; budget Opus for this era. See
[PROTOTYPES.md](PROTOTYPES.md).

## Risk

A flat, register-based sky is at genuine odds with a game built on depth and speed; the discipline
throughout is to translate vertical motion into time and order, never to add modelling. Quadrat
stacking is built, but only for the words the checked vocabulary spells, and the density it buys has
to stay at the margins of the sheet or a wall painted edge to edge would bury the flight. A fan-led
brush with no point and no aerodynamic vane is the one tool on the whole ladder that cannot lean into
a turn the way a nib or a hull can — whether its own silhouette still reads a heading clearly at
speed, on top of the aim guide, is unspiked. Beyond securely documented labels, decan-to-modern-
constellation identifications must not be invented. Several words in the Names table remain recalled
rather than re-verified and should not enter final hieroglyphic prose. The Met facsimile and the AEA
record have been inspected directly; Greenfield frames 85 and 87 remain later comparators, not
sources for TT353 itself. This era's own three-state reveal, its frontier decay, its knowledge
structure and its transition are all plan, not code, until `src/reveal.js` is retargeted from
`world.time` to `orbitSweep` and the observation ledger and transition designation land in
`src/simulation.js` — the spine work [OVERVIEW.md](OVERVIEW.md)'s build order puts before any of it. The special
area's mythology is consistent and clearly bounded, but it remains a designed synthesis rather than a
reconstruction of one intact ancient room.
